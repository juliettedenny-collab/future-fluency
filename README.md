# Future Fluency

**An open framework for staying relevant, and growing, in work shaped by AI.**

## The idea in one line

> **The ten fluencies are the map. Foundational, functional and future are where each person stands on it.**

You are not certified in the skills of the future. You become more or less fluent in them, like a language, and you are never finished.

Most AI skills programmes teach a tool. Tools move. Certify someone in a chatbot and you have certified them in a version; a year later the buttons have moved and the certificate says something that is no longer true. What transfers is harder to see: knowing when to trust a model and when to check it, explaining how an automated decision was reached, bringing a sceptical team with you through a change. Future Fluency gives that a shape you can plan with.

---

## Three layers

### 1. The bedrock: who are you, and can you change?
Beneath any skill sit the capabilities that decide whether you can learn a new one at all: growth mindset, curiosity, creativity, communication and influence, the habit of shipping, and resilience under uncertainty. **Growth mindset is the gate.** A person with the appetite to change can reach any fluency. A person without it cannot be trained into one.

### 2. The map: what does work shaped by AI need?
Ten fluencies, the same map for everyone. They are set out below.

### 3. Your position: where do you stand?
For each fluency, a person is at one of three degrees. The degree is not a property of the skill. **It is the relationship between a fluency and a person**, and it moves.

## The ten fluencies

### Working with the machine
| Fluency | What it means |
|---|---|
| **AI** | Working with AI as a thinking partner: prompting, integrating it into real work, and knowing what it can and cannot do. |
| **Data** | Turning data into understanding, and above all into a story that moves a decision. |
| **Automation** | Engineering AI into operations: deciding what to automate, orchestrating it, and redesigning the process around it. |
| **Frontier** | Keeping up with what comes next, and judging which new capabilities matter for your work. |

### Keeping it right
| Fluency | What it means |
|---|---|
| **AI Governance** | Deploying AI responsibly: accountability, fairness, explainability, and knowing who decides. |
| **Trust** | Protecting the systems and data people rely on, and building the human trust that change depends on. |
| **Sustainability** | Building environmental and social responsibility into how work and technology are designed. |

### Judgement and people
| Fluency | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Foresight** | Anticipating what has not happened yet: scenarios, signals, and preparing before you have to. |
| **Change** | Taking people and organisations through change: transition, adoption, and bringing the sceptics with you. |
| **Systems** | Reasoning about connected, non-linear systems, including judging AI's own output. |

Technology only moves as fast as the people who deploy it and the people who use it. That is why the third group matters as much as the first.

## The three degrees

| Degree | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Already fluent. Table stakes this person arrived with. |
| **Functional** | Working fluency the role uses now, every day. |
| **Future** | Coming for this role, but the person is not there yet. |

**"Is this a future skill?" has no answer until you ask: whose future?**

### Worked example: one fluency, three people
Take **AI fluency**.
- For a **finance director**, it is *future*: a growth edge still ahead.
- For a **data analyst**, it is *functional*: part of the job today.
- For a **graduate** who grew up with these tools, it is *foundational*: an assumed baseline.

Same fluency, three people, three degrees, at one moment in time.

### Worked example: two people, two fluencies, the flip
Take a **senior clinician** with twelve years of brilliant patient work, and a **sales adviser** with one year in the job.

| | Senior clinician | Sales adviser |
|---|---|---|
| **AI** | *Future.* Nobody has yet shown her where AI fits inside a real appointment. | *Functional.* Already uses it every day, at home and at work. |
| **Trust** | *Functional.* Helping a nervous customer understand what a new tool is doing is what she does all day. | *Future.* Has not yet had to explain an automated decision to someone worried by it. |

Each is strong where the other is still growing. So the plan is different for each: stretch the adviser on Trust, show the clinician AI inside her real work, and let each help the other. That is what makes the framework plannable.

## Two halves: what a qualification can teach, and what it never will

Sort the ten by whether a qualification can actually teach them, and a fault line appears.

- **The certifiable half:** AI, Data, AI Governance, Automation, Sustainability and Frontier. A course or qualification can take people a long way here.
- **The human half:** Foresight, Change, Systems and the relational core of Trust. No exam evidences that you can hold your nerve through a reorganisation, read a complex system, or bring a sceptical team with you. These are grown by doing the job, with guidance, in role.

The fluencies the AI economy rewards most are in the human half, and they are the ones our skills systems are least built to grow.

## Using Future Fluency in a transformation

1. **Start with the job.** Pick the roles the change touches. Map how the work is actually done today, not how the org chart says it is done.
2. **Place people on the map.** For each role, mark the likely degree on each fluency. Then check it: ask people what they actually did, not how they feel.
3. **Choose the few that matter.** A transformation is a handful of people moving a handful of fluencies from *future* to *functional*, on purpose. Name them per role.
4. **Grow them in the work.** Practise on real tasks, with feedback, a little at a time. Use champions: people adopt what they see a colleague do.
5. **Measure what changed.** Report three things: movement (how many people moved a fluency from future to functional, by role), behaviour in the work, and the business outcome you agreed before you started.

> Completion tells you who clicked. It does not tell you who can.

## Where it came from

The ten fluencies emerged from an analysis of the emerging-skill signals in **58,802 UK role descriptions**, clustered into stable domains, and were then read against independent findings from the World Economic Forum, LinkedIn and PwC, which corroborate the shape. It is offered as a well-evidenced hypothesis about where demand is converging: a lens, not a decree.

Future Fluency sits behind new Level 3 and Level 5 diplomas.
